Diffusion for Dummies

A visual, intuitive, math-grounded guide to how diffusion models turn noise into images.

Tip: in the forward process slider, t = 0 is a clean image and t = 1 is full noise.
Quick navigation Forward · Reverse · Training · Sampling · Latents

1. Why Diffusion?

We make generation easy by breaking it into tiny denoising steps. First, we corrupt an image with Gaussian noise (the forward process). Then we learn to reverse those steps (the reverse process).

Reverse intuition: slide right = remove noise.

2. The Forward Process

We corrupt a clean image \(x_0\) at step \(t\) via

\( x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I) \)

Here \( \beta_t \) is a small “noise rate” per step, \( \alpha_t = 1-\beta_t \), and \( \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s \). Larger \(t\) ⇒ more noise.
Schedule: t = 0
Plot of \( \bar\alpha_t \) (amount of signal kept). It decreases as noise increases.

3. The Reverse Process (Toy)

The model learns \( \varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t) \) so it can step toward a cleaner \(x_{t-1}\). Below, a simple toy denoiser (box blur) stands in for the learned network to show the iterative feel:

\( x_{t-1} \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\!\left(x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\;\hat\varepsilon(x_t,t)\right). \)

4. Training Objective

We train by predicting the noise we added:

\( \mathcal L_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb E_{x_0,t,\varepsilon}\big[\|\varepsilon - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2\big]. \)

for x0 in dataloader:
  t = torch.randint(0, T, (B,))
  eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
  xt = sqrt_alphabar[t]*x0 + sqrt_one_minus_alphabar[t]*eps
  pred = model(xt, t)
  loss = F.mse_loss(pred, eps)

5. Sampling (DDPM)

x = torch.randn((1,3,64,64))
for t in reversed(range(T)):
  z = torch.randn_like(x) if t > 0 else 0
  eps = model(x, t)
  x = (x - coef(t)*eps)/sqrt(alpha[t]) + sigma(t)*z
Classifier-free guidance (CFG) mixes conditional and unconditional predictions to steer samples without an explicit classifier.

6. Latent Diffusion

Stable Diffusion runs the same math in the autoencoder’s latent space \(z\) (much smaller than pixels), then decodes back to the image. That’s why it’s fast.

7. Try It Yourself

from diffusers import StableDiffusionPipeline
pipe = StableDiffusionPipeline.from_pretrained("runwayml/stable-diffusion-v1-5")
pipe.to("cuda")
image = pipe("a fox in a misty forest", num_inference_steps=25, guidance_scale=7.5).images[0]
image.save("out.png")
